# Slot A thread: Figma and AI generators

**Status:** READY · **Post at:** 09:30 IST (suggested) · **Source:** [the essay](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/if-a-designer-can-fully-replace-figma-with-ai-generators-and-lose-nothing-e086a93da73e)

## Tweet 1 (261 chars) · image: `images/hook.png`

If a designer can swap Figma for an AI generator and lose nothing, were they designing in the first place?

I saw that on LinkedIn and it sounded harsh. Then I kept hearing people who vibe-code their screens say "honestly, I didn't lose much by skipping Figma."

## Tweet 2 (262 chars)

The final screen is the easiest part of design. Once you know what it should look like, building it is production work.

The thinking happens earlier, in the messy middle. You open a blank frame, try a layout, scrap it, and build ten versions of the same screen.

## Tweet 3 (265 chars)

AI generators are very good at that production stage. Give them a clear direction and they assemble a polished screen quickly.

They generate options, but they don't search for the right one. They don't sit with ambiguity or test an idea against intent and context.

## Tweet 4 (202 chars, URL counted as 23)

So if skipping Figma cost you nothing, maybe the exploring was never part of your process.

AI can generate many answers. Deciding which one matters is still design.
